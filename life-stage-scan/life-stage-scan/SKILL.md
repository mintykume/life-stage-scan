# Life Stage Scan

Most life-audit prompts take what a person says about themselves at face value and hand back a generic 90-day plan. This skill reads a person in layers: their circumstances, how they're built, what they're reaching for, how they make meaning, and the story they tell about themselves. It pays most attention to where those layers disagree, because that's where the real stage shows. The reading then becomes:

- one best next move
- a way to contribute from exactly where they stand
- ideas that make everyday life feel richer (rituals, style, things to explore)
- a plan with horizons

## Principles

- **Hypotheses, not verdicts.** Every claim about the person carries its evidence and a confidence level. People are more than any framework; the frameworks here are lenses for noticing, not boxes to sort into.
- **Behavior outranks self-description.** What someone does with free time, money and attention says more than what they say they value. When the two disagree, the gap is data.
- **The deepest finding usually lives between layers.** A person whose story says "I'm the responsible one" but whose energy spikes only when they break rules is in a different stage than either layer alone suggests.
- **Stage isn't rank.** Exploring at 30 isn't failure; caring deeply about family expectations isn't immaturity. Read the stage to find its task, not to grade the person.
- **Advance one, maintain the rest.** The plan pushes one thing forward hard and keeps everything else at a sustainable minimum. A plan that improves everything at once is abandoned by week two.
- **Honest, kindly delivered.** If every layer reads as good news, look again. Name the uncomfortable pattern gently, with evidence and a way forward.
- **The world is checked, not remembered.** Look up anything about the world as it is now (job markets, programs, scholarships, deadlines, prices, visa or legal rules) before it shapes a recommendation. Give any project or contribution idea a quick check for whether it already exists and what this person could add.
- **Fun is load-bearing.** Rituals, style and adventures aren't decoration. They make the plan livable and the chapter worth being in.

## Step 0: Start from what's already known

Before asking anything, gather what's available: this conversation, memory, past chats, files the person shared. Ask only for what's missing. Re-asking what someone already said is the fastest way to make a "deep" scan feel shallow.

## Step 1: Choose the depth

- **Quick read:** enough context exists, or the person wants speed. Ask at most three sharp questions, then read.
- **Full scan (default):** two or three rounds of questions, each shaped by the last answers.
- **Re-read:** there's an earlier reading. Compare: what moved, what didn't, which hypotheses held up.

Don't ask which mode they want. Pick from context and start; they can redirect.

## Step 2: Intake

Use `references/question-bank.md` for the full question set, what each question reveals, and follow-up patterns. In short:

- **Round 1, Snapshot:** life phase, what fills a typical week (their life roles), what's in motion, real constraints, and why they're asking now.
- **Round 2, Deep scan:** four to six questions chosen from the bank. Topics: role models, chosen media, a favorite story, self-advice, revealed preferences, envy, recurring complaints, high and turning points, whose voice decides, readiness to change.
- **Round 3, Threads:** ask about the contradictions you noticed. This round is what makes the scan deep; skip it only in a quick read.

Ask in plain text, numbered, about five questions per round. Say that short answers, voice notes and skipping are all fine. Painful history is never required; if it comes up, acknowledge it and don't mine it for content.

## Step 3: The layered read

Read six layers. `references/layers.md` has the signals for each, framework notes, and common misreads. Citations and how strong each framework's evidence is are in `references/sources.md`; be honest about those limits if the person asks.

1. **Season & situation:** life roles and their weight, career season, transition phase, resources and constraints.
2. **Build:** temperament and energy, read from behavior rather than test labels.
3. **Strivings:** goals, values, skills, coping, readiness per goal, and the capital already built.
4. **Meaning-making:** whose standards run the show (socialized, self-authoring, self-transforming). Always a light-touch hypothesis.
5. **Story:** narrative identity, recurring themes, redemption versus contamination, whose script they're living, the life theme.
6. **Tensions:** where the layers disagree. Pick the one or two tensions that explain the most.

Then synthesize the **Stage Reading**:

- **Chapter name:** evocative but accurate.
- **What this chapter is for:** its real developmental task, in one paragraph.
- **The core tension:** what's under the surface.
- **The leverage point:** the single move that shifts several layers at once.

Label evidence inline:

- **[said]** they told you
- **[shown]** behavior they described
- **[read]** your inference

Give each a confidence level (low, medium or high). This is what keeps a deep reading from turning into a horoscope.

## Step 4: Paths and the best next move

Sketch three paths, after the odyssey-plan exercise in Burnett & Evans' *Designing Your Life*:

- **A, Deepen:** the current trajectory, done well.
- **B, Pivot:** what they'd do if path A vanished tomorrow.
- **C, Wild card:** what they'd do if money and other people's opinions didn't matter.

Score each path 1–5 on these criteria:

- **Fit** with the layers.
- **Compounding:** does it build rare skills, proof and options?
- **Reversibility.**
- **Contribution:** who else benefits.
- **Aliveness:** energy and joy.
- **Cost:** time, money, and what it rules out.

Show the scores briefly, then name the winner and why. Often the best move borrows from path C to make A or B more alive.

Choose **one** primary move (advance one). State what each other area needs at minimum (maintain the rest).

Before recommending anything that depends on current conditions, search the web. For a contribution or project idea, check what already exists. If there's a gap, say where this person's angle is new. If there isn't, point them to join or build on what's there.

**Hand-offs**, when these skills are available:

- How they stand in one specific field: `where-i-stand`.
- Which project to build: `project-navigator`.
- Learning a topic properly: `book-mode`.

## Step 5: How they contribute from here

Nobody needs to be established to contribute; the form changes with the season.

- **Rare combination:** two or three skills, experiences or worlds that are unusual together. Contribution is often translation between worlds few people stand in at once.
- **One step behind:** who is where they were a year or two ago? They can help those people now.
- **Smallest public form:** document, teach, build a small tool, organize a gathering, translate, curate.

What contribution looks like in each season:

- **Explorers:** learn in public and help peers.
- **Establishers:** ship things others use.
- **Maintainers:** systematize and sponsor others.
- **Reinventors:** bridge their old and new worlds.

## Step 6: Life texture

Pick the two or three modules that matter most for this person. `references/life-texture.md` has the full guidance for each.

- **Romanticize:** rituals and small adventures built from their own story, grounded in savoring research (anticipating, being in, and reminiscing about good experiences). Always include free versions.
- **Personal style:** the costume for the next chapter. Give a three-word style statement, a palette, silhouette principles that work with the body as it is, and one piece to add and one to retire.
- **Explore & learn:** one compounding skill, one curiosity topic, three verified culture picks, and one new environment.
- **On request:** environment, people and community, energy, money basics.

## Step 7: The plan

| Horizon | Contents |
|---|---|
| Today | one move of 15 minutes or less that creates momentum |
| This week | three actions |
| 30 days | the main quest (the one advance), one or two side quests, one ritual |
| 90 days | a milestone plus a proof artifact: something that exists afterwards |
| This year | a direction, not a fixed target |

For each item, give:

- the *why* (which layer it serves)
- the smallest version
- a signal that it's working
- a signal to change course

Add a **Stop / pause** list; subtraction usually frees more energy than any addition.

Close with:

- **Three questions to sit with.**
- **What would change this reading:** the information that would flip the recommendation.

Offer a revisit: a re-read in a month, or a recurring check-in if the session can schedule tasks.

## Output

- **Quick read:** in chat, short.
- **Full scan:** a document the person can keep (a doc artifact where available).

Follow `references/output-template.md`. For tone and density, see the fictional worked example in `references/example-reading.md`.

Write in the person's language and register. Be vivid, but trace every recommendation to something they said or showed. If a line could be pasted into anyone else's reading, cut it or make it specific.

## Care and boundaries

- **Not therapy.** This is reflection and planning, not therapy or diagnosis. Don't assign mental-health conditions, disorders or clinical labels.
- **Distress first.** If the person is in acute distress, grieving or in crisis, put the framework down. Respond to them as a person and point toward real support.
- **Sensitive attributes.** Don't infer health, sexuality, religion or similar attributes. Use them only if the person raises them and wants them considered.
- **Duty can be chosen.** Family duty, faith and community obligations can be chosen values. Don't read them as automatic signs of a "socialized" mind.
- **High-stakes decisions.** For money, legal, visa or medical decisions, give information and options, note that you're not a licensed advisor, and point to qualified people when the stakes are high.
- **Minors.** If the person seems to be under 18, keep it age-appropriate and centered on school, interests and friendships. Suggest bringing trusted adults into big decisions.
