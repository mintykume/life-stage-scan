# Question bank

Use this file during Step 2 (Intake). Choose questions; don't send all of them. About five per round, numbered, in plain text. Tell the person that short answers, voice notes and skipping are all fine.

## How to ask

- **One round at a time.** Let each round's answers shape the next. A long form gets answered on autopilot; a short round gets real answers.
- **Concrete over abstract.** "When did you last lose track of time?" beats "What are your passions?"
- **Mirror their register.** Ask playfully with a playful person and plainly with a plain one. Use their language.
- **Explain sparingly.** One clause saying why you're asking is enough, and only for the questions that might feel odd (role models, favorite story).
- **Skip what's already known.** If the conversation or memory already answers a question, don't ask it.

## Round 1: Snapshot

These are facts, quick to answer.

1. **Life phase.** Roughly where you are: studying, early in work, mid-career, raising kids, between things, later in life.
2. **A typical week.** What fills it: study, work, caring for someone, side projects, hobbies, community. These are their life roles; notice which ones they list first.
3. **What's in motion.** Anything changing right now: a move, a new job, the end of something, a decision pending.
4. **Real constraints.** Time, money, location, obligations, energy.
5. **Why now.** What made you ask today? This often names the real question.

## Round 2: Deep scan

Choose four to six, based on what Round 1 left unclear.

| # | Question | What it reveals |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who did you admire growing up: up to three people, real or fictional? What exactly did you admire? | The self they're building. Role models act as a blueprint (Savickas, Career Construction Interview). |
| 2 | What do you read, watch or scroll without anyone telling you to? | The settings and problems they're drawn to. |
| 3 | What's a favorite story (book, film, game or show)? Tell me the plot in a few lines. | The script they may be trying to live. Notice which part of the plot they emphasize. |
| 4 | What's a saying or line you tell yourself when things get hard? | Their self-advice. It often contains the next step they already know. |
| 5 | When did you last lose track of time? | Revealed energy and intrinsic motivation. |
| 6 | What do people ask you for help with? | Capital others already see, even when the person doesn't. |
| 7 | What do you spend money on without guilt? | Revealed values. |
| 8 | Whose life are you a little envious of, and which specific part? | An unmet want, named more precisely than "goals" usually are. |
| 9 | What do you complain about again and again? | A preoccupation that could become an occupation or a contribution. |
| 10 | Give me a high point and a turning point from the last few years. | Story structure: redemption or contamination, agency or communion. |
| 11 | If this chapter of your life had a title, what would it be? | Their own framing. Compare it with yours later. |
| 12 | When you got conflicting advice on a big decision, how did you finally choose? | Whose voice decides (meaning-making layer). |
| 13 | Whose disappointment would hurt most? | Whose standards they're carrying. |
| 14 | What would you do this year if nobody you know would ever find out? | The self-authored want, with the audience removed. |
| 15 | For the one thing you most want to change: are you thinking about it, preparing, doing it, or keeping it going? | Readiness (stages of change). The plan must match it. |

**Never required:** low points, trauma or painful history. If something painful comes up, acknowledge it in a sentence. Don't treat it as material for the reading unless they explicitly want it considered.

## Round 3: Threads

Ask about what didn't fit. Patterns:

- **Stated versus revealed.** "You said X matters most, but your free time goes to Y. Tell me about that."
- **Role model versus current path.** "Everyone you admired worked for themselves; you've chosen two very structured paths. How do you hold that?"
- **Story versus evidence.** "You call yourself 'not creative,' and then described making three things this month."
- **Envy versus plan.** "The part you envy is control over your days. Nothing in your plan touches that. Is that deliberate?"
- **Readiness gap.** "You've been 'thinking about' this for two years. What would 'preparing' look like?"

Two or three thread questions are enough. Ask them with curiosity, not as a gotcha; the person often already knows the answer and has never been asked.

## Reading the answers

- **What they skip** is data. Skipped questions often guard the live issue. Note them without pressing.
- **Verbs.** "I have to" versus "I get to" versus "I'm supposed to."
- **Who appears** in their stories. Some stories are all agency (me, my achievements); some are all communion (us, the people I love).
- **Energy in the writing.** Long, detailed answers to one question and one-word answers to another mark where the life is.
- **Their chapter title against yours.** The gap between the two is often the core tension.

## Adapting

- **Quick read:** pick the three questions that would most change the reading. Usually that's "why now," "lose track of time" and "whose voice decides."
- **Re-read:** ask what changed since last time, what they tried, what surprised them, and whether any part of the last reading felt wrong.
- **Younger people:** frame questions around school, friends, hobbies and what they'd like to learn. Skip money and career-capital questions unless they raise them.
- **Culture:** family and community expectations can be chosen values. Ask how they feel about an obligation before reading it as pressure.
