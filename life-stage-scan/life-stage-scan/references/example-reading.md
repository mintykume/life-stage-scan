# Worked example (fictional)

This is an invented person, used to show the tone, density and evidence labeling of a full scan. Don't reuse the content. Match the method.

**Context gathered over two rounds of questions.** Teo is 27 and a junior accountant at a mid-sized firm in Valencia. He's three years in and studying for a professional certification his parents encouraged. On weekends he films 60-second videos about the history of old buildings in his neighborhood and posts them to an account with about 400 followers. He sings in a community choir.

- **Role models:** his grandfather, who restored furniture; a history teacher who "made the city feel alive."
- **Favorite story:** a film about a bus driver who writes poems in a notebook and doesn't show anyone.
- **Envy:** a creator who explains architecture — "not the views, the fact that people want to hear him talk."
- **Last conflicting-advice decision:** he followed his father.
- **Readiness for "doing more with the videos":** "thinking about it for two years."

---

## 1. Your Chapter: *Reliable, Rehearsing in Secret*

You're in the establishing season of one career and the quiet early exploration of another. The task of this chapter isn't choosing between accounting and explaining. It's to stop rehearsing in private, and let the reliable person you already are run the creative work in public, small and on a schedule.

## 3. Under the Surface

Your story is about *making others see the value in old things* [read, medium]. That is exactly what your grandfather did with furniture and your teacher did with the city [said]. But your favorite story is about someone who keeps his work hidden [said], and your videos are your most alive hours yet stay at the edge of your week [shown]. The tension isn't accounting versus art. It's being visible: you envy being listened to [said] and you've built a life where no one has to listen.

## 4. The Leverage Point

Make your reliability work for the hidden thing. Commit to a **public, weekly, small** format, so the trait your family trusts in you carries the work they don't yet see.

## 5. Three Paths & the Best Next Move

| | A: Deepen | B: Pivot | C: Wild card |
|---|---|---|---|
| Path | Finish certification, specialize in real-estate or heritage-property finance | Move into heritage education: museum programs, walking tours | Full-time explainer channel on cities and buildings |
| Fit | 3 | 4 | 4 |
| Compounding | 4 | 3 | 3 |
| Reversibility | 5 | 3 | 2 |
| Contribution | 2 | 4 | 4 |
| Aliveness | 2 | 4 | 5 |
| Cost (5 = cheap) | 4 | 3 | 1 |

**Best next move:** A, made alive by C. Keep the job and the exam at maintenance level. For 12 weeks, publish one 60-second video every Sunday in a series: *the money behind this building* — who paid for it, why it was built, why the ground floor is now a phone shop. Your accounting knowledge is the angle most architecture explainers don't have.

**Maintain:** exam study at 4 hours a week (enough not to lose ground), choir as is, sleep protected on Sunday nights.

**Checked:** before suggesting the angle, look up existing local-history accounts in the city to confirm the money angle is underserved, and link them. (In a real reading these links appear here.)

## 6. How You Contribute From Here

- **Rare combination:** accounting + local history + short-form video. You can explain the economics of a street, which almost nobody does.
- **One step behind you:** juniors studying for the same exam. A monthly "exam in plain words" post costs you little and builds trust.
- **Smallest public form:** the Sunday series. Later, one walking tour for colleagues.

## 7. Life Texture

**Romanticize.**

- **Sunday field walk:** same route start, a different street each week, camera and notebook only [ties to: grandfather's restoring, shown love of old things].
- **Anticipation:** pick next week's building on Monday and write its name on a card on your desk.
- **Reminiscing:** a "building log": one line per building, what surprised you.
- **Free version:** all of it.

**Style: "archivist with a pulse."**

- **Textures:** wool, corduroy, canvas.
- **Palette:** earthy, mid-value; test olive, rust and navy near your face in daylight before buying.
- **Add:** one field jacket with good pockets, which works on camera and at the office on Fridays.
- **Retire:** the shirts that don't fit since last year.

**Explore & learn.**

- **Compounding skill:** short-form scriptwriting (the hook in the first 3 seconds).
- **Wonder topic:** the history of your neighborhood's central market.
- **Culture:** *Paterson* (Jim Jarmusch, 2016); *How Buildings Learn* (Stewart Brand); *The Death and Life of Great American Cities* (Jane Jacobs).

## 9. Questions to Sit With

1. Who taught you that the good things should be kept in a notebook?
2. If your grandfather saw the videos, what would he point at first?
3. What would "following your father's advice" look like if the advice were about restoring, not careers?

## 10. What Would Change This Reading

- If the exam is a hard requirement for staying employed this year, shift the main quest to the exam and keep the videos at two a month.
- If the videos are fun *because* they're private, the visibility tension is wrong. The leverage point becomes depth (a longer project), not reach.
